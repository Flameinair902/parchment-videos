# explainmethisstory playbook

Rewritten by every scheduled run after it refreshes the numbers. Rules here steer topic, hook, title, pacing and captions.
Evidence counts only posts over 20 h old. A rule moves to **Do** or **Avoid** only with at least 2 supporting posts; one-off signals stay under **Watching**.

Last updated: 2026-11-18 (12 posts with Instagram metrics, 8 with YouTube views)

## What the numbers say so far
- Instagram views track skip rate closely. Best: Tea Race 1,309 views (skip 0.33, 13.4 s avg watch), Sun is tiny 230 (0.41), trees vs stars 196 (0.29, 12.5 s). Worst: smoke alarm 116 (skip 0.75, 4.9 s), Bligh 122 (0.55), godwit 140 (0.62).
- YouTube is a different audience: two Shorts broke out (Tea Race "A 99-day race decided by minutes" 675, DNA "Your DNA once cost $95 million to read" 639); the rest got under 40.
- Average watch time is 5 to 13 s on 20 to 32 s videos: most viewers leave before the halfway point.
- Interactions are near zero on every post; the closing caption questions are not working.

## Do
- Hooks with **stakes or a contest and a concrete number** ("99 days, decided by minutes"; "once cost $95 million"). These are the only two breakouts, on both platforms.
- Hooks that **pit two familiar things against each other** or flip an assumption (more trees than stars; our Sun looks tiny). Lowest skip rates.
- Put the first visible change on screen within 1 s, and land the first real payoff (the number, the reveal) by about 6 to 8 s, since average watch is under 13 s.
- YouTube titles: a short curiosity line with a number or stakes.

## Avoid
- Hooks built on one subject the viewer has never heard of (a named bird, a named sailor, a named turtle): skip 0.51 to 0.62, low views on both platforms.
- Warning or danger framing about the home ("radioactive metal on your ceiling"): worst skip so far (0.75).

## Watching (one data point, not a rule yet)
- Tea Race went out around 09:00 UK time and was the longest video (32 s); all others ran at 12:30 or 18:30. Morning time and a longer, more narrative piece are untested.
- Exploded style opens on a small grey object: possibly a weak first frame. Card antenna (2026-11-18) will test this again.
- Ending with a specific prompt ("Guess before the end", "Which would you pick?") instead of a generic question.
